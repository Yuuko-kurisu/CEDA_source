# Data-generation procedures

These notes describe the available source-checked generation procedures. The original generators and all of their runtime inputs are not bundled as a verified, end-to-end executable workflow. The scripts exposed at the repository root reproduce saved evaluation statistics instead.

## SEEK-Sim and node-level experiments

The inspected SEEK-Sim source constructs a synthetic industrial-process graph, allocates and injects configured relation defects, and produces sensor trajectories and known defect labels. Expert-action labels are then generated by predefined rules associated with process conditions and injected configurations. These are synthetic rule-based labels, not observations of human operators or human scoring. The saved ground-truth node labels originate from the injected configurations and provide the evaluation reference.

In the inspected generation path, the sensor sequence is constructed before the expert-action labels; an expert action does not update the next sensor state through an executed plant-transition step. This statement describes that generator path and does not characterize the separate controlled-SCM or BSM plants.

The source also generates synthetic performance fields; the `performance.sd` label is derived from the corresponding synthetic performance difference, and is not interchangeable with the expert-versus-system action-disagreement indicator.

The five later strict-protocol Medium experiments have saved trajectory-index partitions and training-only scaler records. These do not reconstruct the historical sample membership of Tables III, V, or IX. In the inspected five-case archives, explicit `a_sys` and `r_t` fields were absent and the data loader used its recorded system-action fallback. This scope should be considered when interpreting residual activation in these five cases.

## Edge-defect cases

The edge-defect generator uses configured omitted, spurious, and weakened relations. Its documented fallback trajectory rule uses injected ground-truth edge endpoints to choose the sensor variables from which synthetic action labels are constructed. This is a deliberately constructed benchmark rule, not an independently sampled human-expert policy. The node/edge annotations are evaluation labels; their generation source should not be confused with a direct model input.

## Controlled SCM

The controlled SCM includes a simulated plant state transition in which control actions affect the next state. Known true and corrupted graph configurations support structural and contextual diagnostics. The reported channel-necessity scores are statistical diagnostics; they should not be described as full neural CCI training results. The available archive records the data underlying Table IV and the earlier scale analysis. The 256-node controlled-SCM extension has been completed separately and is not included in this review archive.

## BSM repair evaluation

The BSM plant steps forward with control inputs. The archived 30-case repair benchmark has fixed candidate and repair-application rules, including a reference corrected graph for the ground-truth repair. Its supervised repair ranker uses statistical behavioral and graph features, rather than the SEEK-Sim neural CCI scores. The released table scripts recalculate stored repair-selection and utility summaries, not fresh BSM rollouts.

## Randomness and data partitions

Available configurations and seeds are recorded in `configs/` and `seeds/`. The BSM grouping record and saved strict-protocol splits are in `splits/`. Some original historical per-sample split identifiers and full execution commands were not retained; later splits are not replacements for those records. Consult `REPRODUCIBILITY.md` for the exact supported scope.
